# 07 — Content, Sources, and Publication Governance

**Version:** 0.6  
**Date:** 2026-11-15  
**Status:** Conceptual rules with accepted product directions, proposed workflows, and open decisions.

## 1. Purpose and status

The main audience is the curious general public. People should discover connected history, stories, people, companies, organizations, and teams through meaningful default charts and understandable story threads. Each initial visual view is accompanied by a short essential overview; context, detailed explanations, and sources can be opened when useful. Readers should not have to behave like specialist researchers to enjoy the product.

The accepted direction combines public visual discovery, different real and fictional contexts, optional private spaces, and image/video galleries. Sources and publication controls support that experience. They help the product explain what a connection means without turning each first view into an exhaustive academic record.

Rules `GOV-001` through `GOV-020` retain their previous identifiers. Detailed workflows remain **Proposed** unless specifically labelled **Accepted**. New rules clarify the latest user direction. No content workflow, media pipeline, or moderation feature is claimed to be implemented.

## 2. Public reading and controlled contributions

**GOV-001 — Proposed:** Published public content can be read, searched, visually explored, and compared without an account. Signing in supports drafts, correction proposals, saved collections, and personal activity. Public access does not imply unrestricted anonymous editing. The exact account requirements for reporting and saving remain an experience decision.

**GOV-002 — Proposed:** Permissions depend on role and content scope. Initial roles can be reader, contributor, subject reviewer, and publisher. Contribution history may simplify review, but author reputation does not replace source assessment. Publication authority cannot override personal consent or privacy.

| Role | Proposed authority |
|---|---|
| Reader | Browse public content and submit a traceable issue report. |
| Contributor | Prepare drafts, attach sources, and propose corrections or entities. |
| Subject reviewer | Assess evidence, time, relationship meaning, work edition, and narrative quality. |
| Publisher | Publish, restore, or withdraw material within delegated scope. |

Organizational representatives should be identified in that role. Verifying an account's affiliation with a company does not verify every statement it proposes.

## 3. Publication and correction lifecycle

**GOV-003 — Proposed:** The main lifecycle is draft, submit for review, review, and publish. Reviewers can request changes, reject with reasons, or approve. A pending proposal does not silently alter the public version. Fast correction of a low-impact mistake may be permitted within defined authority and recorded history.

**GOV-004 — Proposed:** Published items can be marked as needing review, disputed, corrected, or withdrawn. Withdrawal explains its reason and effect on dependent narratives and charts. Historical traceability must not republish private information or material removed from public access.

**GOV-005 — Proposed:** Meaningful changes retain before/after differences, contributor, reviewer, time, reason, and sources. A corrected date or relationship propagates to live views using that statement. A referenceable version indicates its last review and current correction status. Saved views distinguish live updates from a version selected for reference.

## 4. Statements, evidence, and provenance

**GOV-006 — Proposed:** Sources attach to specific statements, not only to the bottom of a page. “Played for this team,” “is this character's child,” and “this event happened within this range” are separate statements that may use different evidence. A graph connection can open its explanation and evidence without requiring that detail to occupy the initial view.

A statement should preserve:

- Its subject, relationship or property, and asserted value.
- The applicable real or fictional context and temporal expression, where relevant: event, duration, ordering, unknown time, precision, and uncertainty.
- Its source, evidence location where possible, and access or retrieval date.
- Contributor, extraction method, review state, and reason for selecting a particular interpretation.
- System-recording time, separately from the time the statement describes.

**GOV-007 — Proposed:** Source metadata includes title, creator, publisher or provider, date, language, edition, URL, and evidence location such as page, chapter, verse, or video timestamp. A lost source is not silently discarded: access status and last observation are retained. Keeping a complete source copy depends on its recorded permissions and storage policy.

**GOV-008 — Proposed:** Distinguish a source's direct statement, a contributor's interpretation, an estimate or historical mapping, and an automated suggestion. AI or other extraction identifies its method and is reviewed before publication. Tools must not fabricate dates or relationships to fill a timeline or make a story smoother.

## 5. Confidence, disagreement, and ranking

**GOV-009 — Proposed:** Evidence status and presentation rank are separate. A popular or prominently displayed item does not automatically become more credible. Account standing, historical importance, popularity, editorial selection, and confidence in a statement have distinct meanings and explanations.

**GOV-010 — Proposed:** Prefer explainable labels such as “direct evidence,” “supported by multiple sources,” “approximate,” “disputed,” and “source needed” over unjustified numerical confidence. Sources copying a common text are not automatically independent evidence. Date precision and confidence that an event occurred are different dimensions. Material uncertainty must survive the short overview.

**GOV-011 — Proposed:** Preserve incompatible accounts. Selecting one for the default view needs an explanation and access to alternatives. Merging entities or removing duplicates must not conceal a real disagreement about identity, chronology, or lineage. A brief “accounts differ” indication can lead to the fuller comparison.

## 6. Fictional worlds, editions, and historical mappings

**GOV-012 — Accepted direction; proposed details:** Fictional material such as Shahnameh has an identifiable work and narrative context. Reading order, event order within the story, and the work's composition or publication date are separate. Mapping a fictional person or event to real history is a sourced interpretation.

**GOV-013 — Proposed:** Editions, translations, retellings, and adaptations can differ in characters, names, relationships, and event order. One version must not overwrite another. Comparisons expose the selected context; the reader's chosen account is not presented as the sole account across all versions.

For example, different parentage in two retellings remains attached to each version and its evidence. Missing numeric dates do not prevent storytelling: relative order or a named narrative period can be displayed with the appropriate label. Human plausibility checks are not applied blindly to mythical beings or legendary lifespans. Spoiler settings apply to relationship labels, illustrations, video posters, captions, and recommendations as well as prose.

## 7. Organizations, companies, and memberships

**GOV-014 — Proposed:** Distinguish official company material, independent reporting, self-reported information, and promotional content. An official page identifies its origin; it does not automatically establish independence or neutrality. Relevant financial or organizational connections of contributors are recorded.

**GOV-015 — Proposed:** Membership in a company, board, university, or team is a dated statement. Preserve role, start and end, date status, and source. A role change or departure does not erase the earlier period. A name appearing on one team page does not by itself establish membership for a complete season.

When a newer official source differs from an older report, review both evidence and their dates. “Current chair” requires a last-reviewed context. Exploring a sourced public role is distinct from finding a personal account. A public organization page does not grant access to private history; `PRIV-010` through `PRIV-012` govern personal-account and personal-profile discovery.

## 8. Rights, media, and reuse

**GOV-016 — Proposed:** Imported text or media records its owner or provider, source, permission status, attribution requirements, and permitted uses. The default contribution can be a link, metadata, limited evidence, and an independently written explanation. Importing a whole book, image collection, translation, commentary, or database requires clear, recorded permission for the intended use.

The underlying work, edition, translation, manuscript image, and a website's commentary may have different reuse conditions. Permission for one does not automatically extend to the others. User-contributed media also records provenance and the scope of permission. An unresolved rights status is not treated as publication-ready.

**GOV-017 — Proposed:** Exports, shares, and saved presentations preserve attribution and media restrictions. If material is withdrawn or its permission changes, update affected public copies, indexes, and derivatives controlled by the product. Do not guarantee recall of already downloaded exports or caches outside its control. Make the current status and correction available where permitted.

## 9. Approachable presentation with reliable meaning

**GOV-018 — Accepted direction; proposed details:** Present a short essential explanation first and offer optional expansion into context, detail, alternate accounts, and sources. The overview must preserve the relationship's meaning, important uncertainty, fictional context, and distinction between a modern depiction and historical evidence. Exact text lengths and expansion controls remain design decisions.

**GOV-019 — Accepted direction; proposed details:** Support an enjoyable sequence of related entities and story threads. Explain why a suggested connection exists, for example a shared event, documented membership, depicted character, or common period. A visually appealing connection does not establish causation, kinship, acquaintance, or historical co-occurrence. The editorially selected path is distinct from the complete set of available facts.

**GOV-020 — Proposed:** Review media together with its contextual claims. A photograph's caption, a video's subtitle, the chosen poster frame, and placement in a historical gallery can assert facts even when the accompanying article is accurate. Preserve creator, creation/capture dates, depicted time, source, rights, accessibility content, and any reconstruction or illustration label. The shared rules are specified in [15 — Media and Galleries](15-media-and-galleries.md).

## 10. Acceptance examples

These describe intended future checks, not results from an implemented system.

1. **Reading:** a guest can enjoy a concise public story and open its sources; a correction proposal does not immediately change the public version.
2. **Lineage:** two sources disagree about parentage. Both are preserved and the selected display has an explanation.
3. **Editions:** different adaptations retain separate contextual relationships; merging names does not erase the difference.
4. **Confidence:** popular but weakly sourced material receives no automatic confidence upgrade.
5. **Company:** a departure updates the current role while preserving the documented earlier period.
6. **Automation:** an estimated date remains a suggestion until its source, meaning, and review are established.
7. **Rights:** an image with unresolved reuse permission remains in draft instead of becoming publicly reusable.
8. **Withdrawal:** unreliable evidence triggers review or correction of the statement and dependent story/chart views, with an appropriate explanation.
9. **Short overview:** expanding a simple introduction reveals more context without contradicting the initial summary or concealing a material dispute.
10. **Period gallery:** a newly created illustration of an ancient event is identified as a modern depiction; its placement does not imply that it is contemporary evidence.

See `SC-03`, `SC-14`, `SC-15`, `SC-18`, and `SC-19` in [14 — Reference Scenarios](14-reference-scenarios.md).

## 11. Open decisions

Keep these aligned with the [central decision register](12-decisions-questions-and-change-log.md), especially `Q-02`, `Q-05`, `Q-09`, `Q-15`, `Q-16`, and `Q-17`.

- Which subject areas need specialist review, and which changes can use a lighter process?
- What evidence labels and rules select the default account?
- When do versions share a contextual entity, and when do they need distinct character records?
- What media and data can be exported, and how are reuse restrictions explained?
- How long are rejected proposals, handling records, and withdrawn versions retained?
- Which corrections requested by a living person or organizational representative need independent review?
- How much evidence and uncertainty should appear in the first glance, and what can be expanded without misleading casual readers?

## 12. Implementation input and publication contract

**GOV-021 — Proposed:** Treat an editorial dataset, a local demonstration projection and a published public revision as different artifacts. A sample can be suitable for local design work while specialist, source-edition, rights or accessibility checks remain pending. The initial loader must respect those states rather than interpreting “has sources” or “has a URL” as permission to publish. The implementation handoff defines the bounded first milestone; this distinction does not require building a full authoring workflow before that milestone.

The sample's complete source text fields, ending-sensitive labels and reference URLs are editorial input. Derive a response from the selected context, publication/demo eligibility and reader boundary. Compute its lists, graphs, recommendations and counts from that same eligible set. Show why a link exists using its role and claim; an association does not establish co-presence or a causal relationship. Lowering the reader boundary must also update source labels, navigation history and visible media derivatives.

**GOV-022 — Proposed:** Source descriptions, imported labels and contribution text are data, not executable markup or agent instructions. Render plain text through the framework's safe mechanisms; if rich text is introduced, define and sanitize its allowed format. Validate URL schemes and destinations independently from text escaping. OWASP's [XSS prevention guidance](https://cheatsheetseries.owasp.org/cheatsheets/Cross_Site_Scripting_Prevention_Cheat_Sheet.html) explains why the output context and URL handling require separate controls. Source provenance does not make supplied markup safe.
