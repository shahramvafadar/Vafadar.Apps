# EU law for Dastan – research of 2026-11-19

What EU law asks of the site, as it was looked up on 2026-11-19 for the legal pages and the features that go
with them. It is the record of a research, not legal advice: the owner has the texts checked by a lawyer before
the site is public.

**How far it was verified.** EUR-Lex gave the research tool empty pages, so the articles of the GDPR, of the
ePrivacy Directive and most of the DSA were not read at their source in that session. Each point is marked:

* **[V]** checked on the web in that session (address given);
* **[S]** stable text, quoted from knowledge of the act – no amendment in force was found; to be checked at the

## 1. GDPR

**1.1 The operator is a controller; the household exemption does not help [S].** Art. 4(7); Art. 2(2)(c) with
recital 18 covers purely personal activity only, and publication to an indefinite audience is outside it (CJEU
C-101/01 *Lindqvist*, C-212/13 *Ryneš*, C-25/17 *Jehovan todistajat*). Recital 18, last sentence: the GDPR
applies to controllers "which provide the means" for the household processing of others – a user's private tree
does not take the operator out of scope.

**New, [V via commentary]: CJEU (Grand Chamber) 2 December 2025, C-492/23 *Russmedia*.** A platform operator is
a (joint) controller for personal data in content its users publish, even without knowledge; it cannot use the
hosting safe harbour against duties of the GDPR (Arts 5, 24–26, 32) and has preventive duties under Arts 24/25,
especially for Art. 9 data. The case was about advertisements; whether it reaches all user content is argued.
<https://www.gtlaw.com/en/insights/2026/2/cjeus-russmedia-decision-expands-platform-controller-duties-under-gdpr>,
<https://www.heuking.de/en/news-events/newsletter-articles/detail/cjeu-denies-hosting-safe-harbour-for-gdpr-infringements-russmedia-increases-obligations-for-platform-operators.html>.
*For Dastan:* the gate that is there – a tree is public only when every living person it names has an account
and agreed – is the kind of Art. 25 measure the judgment asks for; the same holds for public photographs.

**1.2 Lawful bases [S].**

| Processing | Basis | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Account (name, password hash, hashed identifier of a provider) | Art. 6(1)(b) | The hashed identifier is pseudonymised and still personal data |
| Server logs, IP addresses, limits per hour, security | Art. 6(1)(f), recital 49 | Dynamic IP addresses are personal data (C-582/14 *Breyer*); short retention, 7–14 days is usual in Germany |
| What a user writes about themself | Art. 6(1)(b) | |
| Relationships between accounts | Consent of both, Art. 6(1)(a) | A partner link can reveal sexual orientation (C-184/20): explicit consent of both, Art. 9(2)(a) |
| Living third persons in a private tree | Art. 6(1)(f) | Balancing; an objection (Art. 21) must be honoured |
| Living persons in a public tree | Art. 6(1)(a), provable (Art. 7(1)) | Withdrawal (Art. 7(3)) must unpublish |
| Photographs of identifiable people, public | Consent of those shown | A tag only with the consent of the tagged account |
| Photographs, private | Art. 6(1)(f) | Not biometric data unless processed to identify (recital 51): no face recognition |
| The dead | Outside the GDPR (recital 27) | Details can still reveal data about living relatives |

**1.3 Special categories (Art. 9) [S].** Trees have no field for it, but free text, captions and photographs can
reveal religion, origin, health, orientation. Measures: no fields for religion, ethnicity or cause of death; a
warning at free-text fields; for the living, name and years only. For public data only Art. 9(2)(a) or (e).

**1.4 Art. 14 for third persons without an account [S].** The duty exists (within a month). Exception
Art. 14(5)(b): impossible or disproportionate effort – credible here, since the operator has no contact data
and collecting it would go against minimisation (Art. 11(1)). Condition: "appropriate measures" – the
information stands in the privacy notice, and a non-user has an easy way to object and to have a record erased.

**1.5 Age [S].** Germany made no derogation from Art. 8: 16. With accounts from 16 the article is not triggered.
No article prescribes a method of checking; expected is a measure in proportion to the risk – a declaration and
removal on discovery (EDPB Statement 1/2025 on age assurance, [U]). German law: 16–17 have limited capacity to
contract (§§ 106 ff. BGB).

**1.6 What a privacy notice holds [S].** Art. 13(1): (a) who the controller is, with contact; (b) the DPO
(none); (c) purposes and legal bases; (d) the legitimate interests where (f) is the basis; (e) recipients;
(f) transfers to third countries and their safeguard. Art. 13(2): (a) how long; (b) the rights – access,
rectification, erasure, restriction, objection, portability; (c) withdrawal of consent; (d) complaint to an
authority; (e) whether giving data is required and what follows from not giving it; (f) automated decisions
(none). Art. 14 adds the categories of data and the source, for third persons. Art. 12(1): plain language –
more so with readers of 16 and 17 (recital 58).

**1.7 Rights without a mail address [S].** Arts 12(2) and 11(2): who cannot be identified has the rights of
Arts 15–20 only after giving what identifies them. Art. 12(6): more may be asked for only on reasonable doubt.
In practice: for an account, the sign-in is the proof – access and a copy, correction, erasure and withdrawal
are done in the account; for a non-user, the operator's address, asking only what ties them to the record.
One month (Art. 12(3)).

**1.8 Records of processing (Art. 30) – required [S].** The exemption for fewer than 250 staff falls away where
processing is "not occasional". A proposal of 21 May 2025 ("Omnibus IV", 2025/0130(COD)) would raise it to 750
and to high risk only; it was in trilogue in June 2026 and is **not in force** [U for November 2026].

**1.9 Impact assessment (Art. 35) [S].** Not clearly mandatory (no case of Art. 35(3), no large scale); two
criteria of WP 248 are met (readers of 16–17, possibly sensitive data, third persons who are not told). After
*Russmedia* a short written one is the cheapest proof of Arts 24/25. The German list of the DSK was not
checked [U].

**1.10 Data protection officer – not required [S]** (Art. 37; § 38 BDSG: 20 persons).

**1.11 Transfers [V for the status].** Art. 27 does not matter (the controller is in the EU). The EU–US Data
Privacy Framework (Decision (EU) 2023/1795) **is valid**: the General Court dismissed the action on 3 September
2025 (T-553/23 *Latombe*); the appeal C-703/25 P (<https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/C/2025/6610/oj>) was pending in
June 2026 and has no suspensive effect; **[U]** whether anything happened since. Transfers to Cloudflare,
Google, Microsoft rest on Art. 45 where the company is certified (<https://www.dataprivacyframework.gov/list>,
not checked); standard contractual clauses (Decision 2021/914) stay in the contract as a fallback.

**1.12 Processors (Art. 28) [S].** A Cloudflare Tunnel ends the encryption at Cloudflare: it sees everything,
passwords included. It is a processor; the contract (its DPA, part of its terms) is mandatory – check and keep
it. The same for a later host. Google and Microsoft are not processors for a sign-in.

**1.13 Breaches (Arts 33/34) [S].** To the authority within 72 hours unless a risk is unlikely; every breach is
written down (Art. 33(5)). With no contact data: a public notice (Art. 34(3)(c)), for example at sign-in.

**1.14 Sign-in through a provider [S].** C-40/17 *Fashion ID* found joint control where a page loads a third
party's element. With a redirect that starts at the visitor's click and nothing of the provider on the pages,
each provider is an independent controller; name them in the notice. Do not load their scripts.

## 2. Storage on a device – Art. 5(3) of Directive 2002/58/EC

**Rule [S]:** storing or reading on a device needs consent unless it is strictly necessary for a service the
user explicitly asked for. Unchanged today; in Germany § 25 TDDDG. EDPB Guidelines 2/2023, version 2.0 of
7 October 2024 [V for existence and date]: any information, any storage, not only cookies; the exemptions are
read with WP29 Opinion 04/2012 and the German "Orientierungshilfe Telemedien".

| Storage | Assessment (after WP29 04/2012) |
|---|---|
| Sign-in cookie | Exempt |
| Cookie against forged forms | Exempt |
| State of a sign-in through a provider | Exempt |
| Language, light or dark | Exempt as a choice of the user, when set by what the user does |
| Reading position, the list | Exempt when the user turned it on; storing progress silently is the weakest case |
| Pages kept by the service worker | Within Art. 5(3). Exempt only as a function the user asked for; keeping every page read by itself is not safe under the narrow German reading, and leaves a reading history on a shared device. **Safer: ask first** |
| Cookies of Cloudflare (`__cf_bm`, `cf_clearance`) | Check whether it sets them; security, arguably exempt, to be named or turned off |

**A banner is not required** where only exempt storage is used. Owed all the same: the information of Art. 13
GDPR – in practice a table of name, purpose and lifetime.

**Reform [V via secondary sources]:** the Commission's Digital Omnibus (19 November 2025, COM(2025) 837) would
move the rules into the GDPR; the Council's mandate of 21 October 2026 reportedly keeps them in the directive
and adds exemptions; the Parliament has no position. **Nothing is in force**, and nothing of it would make the
position of the site stricter.

## 3. Digital Services Act (Regulation (EU) 2022/2065), fully applicable since 17 February 2024

**3.1 Is the site an "information society service" at all? [S; no authority found for a hobby platform].**
Art. 3(a) refers to a service "normally provided for remuneration". *Papasavvas* (C-291/13): the money need not
come from the user. *Mc Fadden* (C-484/14): a free service counts as part of an economic activity. Without
advertising, payment, donations or use of data it is strongly arguable that the site is none – then the DSA,
the Terrorist Content Regulation and Art. 17 of the Copyright Directive do not apply. But: no ruling covers
it; outside the DSA its safe harbour (Art. 6) is not available by its words either, and German courts apply
their general notice-and-takedown liability; donations or sponsorship later would change it.
**Recommendation: keep the basic duties of a host voluntarily** – it costs little and is what German liability
law expects anyway.

**3.2 What binds a one-person provider, if the DSA applies.**

| Article | Applies | Content |
|---|---|---|
| 9, 10 [S] | Yes | Tell an authority what was done about its order; tell the user concerned |
| 11 [S] | Yes | A published point of contact for authorities, with the languages accepted |
| 12 [S] | Yes | A point of contact for users: direct, rapid, electronic, not automated only – a published mail address |
| 13 | No | Established in the EU |
| 14 [S] | Yes | The terms say what users may not post, how content is moderated (measures, tools, human review), how to complain; plain language; significant changes are announced |
| 15 [S] | No | Micro and small enterprises are exempt from reports |
| 16 [V] | Yes | Notice and action, see below |
| 17 [V] | Yes | Statement of reasons, see below |
| 18 [S] | Yes | Tell the police of a suspected crime that threatens life or safety |
| 20–28 [V] | No, except 24(3) | Art. 19 |

**Art. 16 [V, <https://www.eu-digital-services-act.com/Digital_Services_Act_Article_16.html>].** A mechanism
that is easy to reach, electronic, for anybody. It must enable notices with (a) a substantiated explanation,
(b) the exact address, (c) the name and mail address of who gives notice (not for offences of child sexual
abuse), (d) a statement of good faith. (4) Where the notice has contact details: a confirmation of receipt
without undue delay. (5) The decision and the ways of redress are told. (6) Timely, diligent, not arbitrary;
automated means are disclosed. *The site sends no mail by itself; a reply written by the operator satisfies it.*

**Art. 17 [V, <https://www.eu-digital-services-act.com/Digital_Services_Act_Article_17.html>].** For every
removal, hiding or demotion of content and every suspension or ending of an account, for illegality or a breach
of the terms. Contents: (a) what kind of restriction, where, how long; (b) the facts, and whether it followed a
notice or the provider's own look; (c) automated means; (d) the legal ground and why it applies, or (e) the
rule of the terms and why; (f) the ways of redress. (2) Owed only where the provider knows electronic contact
details – without a mail address, the cautious way is to show the statement in the account.

**Art. 19 [V, <https://www.eu-digital-services-act.com/Digital_Services_Act_Article_19.html>].** Arts 20–28 do
not apply to micro and small enterprises, except Art. 24(3): the number of monthly active recipients in the EU
is given to the coordinator (in Germany the Bundesnetzagentur) on request – keep a simple count.

**Art. 28 and the guidelines of 14 July 2025 (OJ C/2025/5519) [V].** Not binding on micro and small
enterprises; a benchmark. Private by default for the young is also what Art. 25(2) GDPR asks.
<https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/C/2025/5519/oj>. EDPB Guidelines 3/2025 on DSA and GDPR, final 17 September 2026

## 5. What a careful lawyer would also name

1. The operator's name, postal address and mail address are public (Art. 13(1)(a) GDPR; § 5 DDG, § 18 MStV).
2. A mail address of the operator is unavoidable, even though the program sends none.
3. Consent for public trees and photographs is provable and, when withdrawn, unpublishes by itself.
4. Defaults (Art. 25(2)): private; not indexed unless chosen; no living third person public without their yes.
5. Cloudflare reads the traffic: in the records, the assessment and the contract.
6. Hosting at home: the security of the machine and its backups is part of Art. 32; a break-in is a breach.
7. A non-user who objects to standing in a private tree: the operator needs a way to remove the record against
   the will of the tree's owner.
8. Accounts with a password only: who loses it and the codes cannot be told apart from a stranger (Art. 11(2)).
9. The young of 16 and 17: plain words; an "EU Kids Act" was reported as announced in September 2026 [U].
